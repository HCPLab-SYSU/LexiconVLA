01Abstract

What if a robot could reuse what it already knows?

Vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle to reuse recurring interactions in unseen tasks. Our diagnostic study reveals that reliable task completion does not imply consistent execution of constituent atomic actions across task contexts. We present LexiconVLA, a retrievable atomic-action lexicon for cross-task reuse.

Global and detail codebooks capture shared interaction structure and fine-grained execution variation, respectively, preserving both reusable patterns and execution details. Visual-Atomic Action Alignment couples trajectory reconstruction from visual state changes with visual outcome prediction from action codes, grounding the lexicon in motion and its effects. We learn these codebooks with trajectory reconstruction and visual alignment on our AtomAction Dataset of 57,803 segments from 69 tasks.

A planner and scene-aware adapter translate new goals into code-conditioned subtasks for a shared policy, without skill-specific experts or deployment-time parameter updates. Across five policy backbones on 26 RLBench tasks, LexiconVLA largely maintains performance on 18 seen tasks while improving success on 8 tasks held out from policy training. With BridgeVLA, unseen-task success rises from 16.67% to 34.17% (+17.50 percentage points), and overall success reaches 71.08%, the highest among methods with reported results. Real-robot experiments demonstrate stepwise execution and failure recovery.

02Motivation

Finishing a task ≠ mastering its parts.

We probe five pretrained manipulation policies of diverse architectures on seven tasks, prompting each atomic subtask alone from its demonstration start state. Task-level training alone does not yield atomic actions that can be reliably invoked on their own — a prerequisite for recombining them in unseen tasks.

Even the contact-free primitive approach varies widely across task contexts within a single policy.

03AtomAction Dataset

One segment, one mechanism.

Most VLA datasets pair a whole demonstration with one instruction, and coarse labels such as open lump together pulling, hinge rotation, and lid flipping. AtomAction instead cuts RLBench expert demonstrations into temporally localized segments that each realize exactly one mechanism-level atomic action.

Trajectory

Low-dimensional state of the phase: end-effector pose, joint positions, velocities and torques, gripper state and contact forces.

Observations

Five-view RGB, depth, and mask observations at the start and end boundaries of the segment.

Language

An atomic-action label plus eight phase-level descriptions written in different language styles.

04Method

A shared vocabulary for robot action.

LexiconVLA conditions one shared manipulation policy on a frozen lexicon of global and detail codes. New goals become sequences of code-conditioned subtasks — no skill-specific experts, no parameter updates at deployment.

  1. 01

    Plan

    A VLM planner decomposes the task instruction into atomic subtask instructions.

  2. 02

    Retrieve

    A scene-aware adapter maps the active subtask and current images to global and detail codes from the lexicon.

  3. 03

    Execute

    A lightweight injection module conditions the shared VLA policy on the retrieved codes while it acts.

Closed-loop scheduling

The planner assesses visual feedback after each step and picks one of four skills. Subtask transitions and replanning refresh the retrieved codes from updated observations.

NEXT

Advance once the active subtask is complete.

CONTINUE

Keep the active subtask when more execution is needed.

RETRY

Repeat a failed step, optionally returning to an earlier subtask.

REPLAN

Regenerate the plan when scene changes invalidate it.

Learning the lexicon

The Vector-Quantized Atomic Action Model (VQAA) grounds every code in both motion and its visual effect. Its decoder is used only during codebook training.

(a) Atomic Action-NSVQ

Global + detail codebooks

A Transformer encodes each 39-D segment; pooling gives one global code for reusable structure, learnable queries give detail codes for execution variation. Noise-substituted VQ and a conditional flow-matching decoder train them by trajectory reconstruction.

(b) Visual-Atomic Action Alignment

V3A: what the action changes

Actions are reconstructed from the visual change between initial and final frames, and final-frame features are predicted from the initial observation and the global code — tying each code to its observable outcome.
